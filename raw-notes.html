Part 2: The Forcing Questions & Diagnostic Pushback

Q1: Who EXACTLY has this problem? (Dismantle "Tenants aged 25–50 in Nigeria")

Refined Location: Kaduna exclusively for Day 1. Abuja is eliminated for MVP.

The Specific Human ("Hajara"): A female NYSC corps member deployed to Kaduna State, posted to GTBank in Barnawa. She is looking for an apartment close to work under a strict 14-day deadline; otherwise, she is stranded and forced to squat with strangers in an unfamiliar city.

Q2: How do you know this problem is real?

Interviews with corps members and relocating software developers revealed concrete, painful friction points:

  • Arbitrary Inspection Fees: Unregulated roadside agents charge ₦2,000–₦5,000 per inspection for substandard apartments.
  • 2-Year Upfront Rent Demands: Landlords demanding 24 months of cash from young professionals with 6–12 months of savings.
  • Discriminatory Bias: Agents and landlords routinely reject single, independent women without male guarantors.
Q3: What happens if Settlla does NOT exist? (The Low-Frequency Challenge)

Tenants rent only once every 1–2 years. To survive:

  • Transaction fee adjusted to 1.5% for low-friction adoption.
  • Agent Subscription Model: Agents pay a monthly subscription to access pre-vetted, active tenant inquiries with verified budgets (paying for qualified demand rather than photo hosting).
Q4: Who else is ALREADY solving this? (Classifieds vs. Trust Engines)
  • Why Jiji & NPC Fail in Kaduna: They are classifieds boards selling visibility boosts. Their incentive is to maximize ad volume, not eliminate duplicate or fraudulent listings.
  • Why WhatsApp Status Competes: Free instant distribution to 50 known contacts. Agents will only pay Settlla if handed net-new qualified demand outside their existing contact book.
  • Proptech Graveyard Lessons: Avoid Fibre/Muster (forced monthly rent), Spleet (RNPL credit default and devaluation risks), and MyPadi (high operational cost of student hostel verification).
  • Kaduna Unit Economics: Lower margins require document-based verification and partnering with the top 10% professional agents/lawyers rather than maintaining expensive field inspection teams.
Q5: What stops customers from saying NO or cutting offline deals?
  • The Settlla Escrow Guarantee (The Shield): Tenant funds are held until the key turns in the door. 100% money back if scammed. 1.5% is marketed as a Move-In Protection & Legal Guarantee Fee.
  • Invisible Landlord Onboarding: Landlords do not download apps. The agent runs the transaction; payout hits the landlord’s traditional bank account via API immediately upon key handover.
  • Agent Mandate & Banishment (The Stick): Exclusive landlord mandates, combined with permanent banishment from high-paying corporate tenant streams if an agent diverts leads offline.
Q6 & Q7: Day-1 Cold-Start Test (The 30-Day Concierge MVP)

Before building software, the founders will execute a 30-day Concierge MVP:

"Before writing a single line of code, our 30-day Concierge MVP will manually close three residential rentals in Barnawa using just WhatsApp, Google Docs, and a corporate bank account... We will physically intercept property inspections to ensure tenants pay through our corporate account rather than cutting direct side-deals. If we can convince tenants to pay a 1.5% premium for the protection of our escrow and legally binding documentation in person, we will have the validated trust blueprint required to build an automated engine."

Part 3: The Reframed Core Foundations

1. Reframed Problem Statement

In Kaduna, incoming professional relocatees (NYSC corps members, tech workers, and corporate bank transferees) face urgent 14-day housing deadlines. They lose money to arbitrary inspection fees (₦2,000–₦5,000/visit), face discrimination (especially single women), and risk losing 100% of their annual rent to ghost agents and unrecovered caution deposits, while reputable property managers struggle to filter out unserious inquiries and manage paper lease agreements.

2. Reframed Target Customer (Dual ICP)

  • Primary Demand ICP ("Hajara"): 22–29 year old female professional/NYSC corps member posted to GTBank in Barnawa, Kaduna. Annual budget ₦400,000–₦800,000, zero local network, high safety concerns, 14-day urgent deadline.
  • Primary Supply ICP ("HB&A Partners"): Licensed property lawyer / formal management firm in Kaduna managing 10–30 properties. Values formal leases, reputable corporate tenants, and clean payment tracking without roadside agent chaos.

3. Reframed Unique Value Proposition (UVP)

For Relocating Professionals: "Rent your next apartment in Barnawa in 7 days with zero inspection fee scams, 100% money-back move-in protection, and a legally binding digital lease—guaranteed."

For Property Lawyers & Managers: "Close verified, corporate-grade tenants faster with automated rent splits directly to your landlords and zero paper leases."

4. Reframed Business & Revenue Model

  • 1.5% Move-In Protection Fee: Paid by the tenant upon closing for digital lease agreements, escrow protection, and move-in fraud indemnity.
  • Agent Qualified Lead Subscription: ₦15,000–₦30,000/month charged to verified property lawyers/managers for access to pre-vetted, active tenant inquiries with confirmed budgets.
  • Caution Vault: Ringfenced virtual sub-accounts via licensed fintech/bank partners preventing landlord personal misappropriation.

Part 4: 30-Day Concierge Action Checklist

  • Days 1–7: Secure Supply Mandates — In-person meetings with HB&A Partners and 2 other Kaduna law firms to secure 5 exclusive property mandates in Barnawa/Malali.
  • Days 8–14: Mobilize Targeted Demand — Partner with Kaduna NYSC orientation coordinators and local tech hubs (CoLab, Blue Sapphire) for direct relocatee outreach.
  • Days 15–22: In-Person Concierge Inspections — Accompany inspections in person as Settlla Trust Representatives to eliminate roadside inspection scams and prevent offline deal diversion.
  • Days 23–30: Execute Closing & Split Payouts — Issue digital lease via Google Docs, collect Rent + Fee + Caution in corporate account, disburse Rent to Landlord and Fee to Agent, and retain Caution in a ringfenced account.
